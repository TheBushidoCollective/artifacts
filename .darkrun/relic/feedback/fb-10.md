**Unit:** `spec-service-surface`. **Criterion failed:** 5 ("Every item in 'What this document must decide' is resolved into a stated rule"). **File:** `docs/spec/service.md` line 213 (section 6, "The cross-origin redirect rule, assigned here by `format.md` 5").

## The defect

`service.md` states the fragment-in-`Location` rule keyed on **origin boundary**. `format.md` and `viewer.md` state it keyed on **trust boundary**, and both explicitly put the two cases that differ on the opposite side. The station therefore ships two contradictory rules for the same redirects, and the app-server implementer reads the wrong one.

`service.md`:213 — "Every redirect the service issues **across an origin boundary** MUST carry an explicit, possibly empty, fragment in `Location` ... and **it bites on HTTP-to-HTTPS upgrade, apex to `www`**, trailing-slash normalization, and any redirect configured in a CDN or load balancer." Its only carve-out is "**A same-origin** canonicalizing redirect is safe on the fragment."

`format.md`:239 — "Staying inside the service, the fragment is **deliberately omitted** so inheritance carries the key through ... That half covers legacy or renamed paths, trailing-slash normalization, apex to `www`, and HTTP to HTTPS; **the last two change origin without leaving Relic**, and an empty fragment on any of them is a deleted key and a recipient on the 'missing its key' screen. **The test is the destination's trust boundary, not its origin tuple.**"

`viewer.md`:88 — identical rule, same words: "**The test is the destination's trust boundary, not its origin tuple.**"

HTTP→HTTPS changes the scheme and apex→`www` changes the host, so both are cross-origin. `service.md`'s same-origin-only carve-out does not reach them, and its own sentence names them as cases where the MUST "bites". So under `service.md` a recipient who types `relic.example/{id}#key` with no scheme, or omits the `www`, gets redirected with an explicit empty fragment, loses the key, and lands on the "link is missing its key" screen holding a link that was never broken. `viewer.md`:88 names this exact outcome as the thing the split exists to prevent: "applying the other half to them would kill the key on every link typed or pasted without a `www` or a scheme."

## Why it is a criterion 5 failure rather than a preference

Section 6's mandated scope statement is a decided item: `spec-viewer` owns redirects Relic itself issues, and this unit owns the cases where the key leaves via somebody else. `service.md`:211 resolves that scope correctly in its opening sentence, then states a rule for the class it just assigned away, in a form that contradicts both the document that assigned it (`format.md` 5) and the document that owns it (`viewer.md` 1.7). An implementer cannot act on a rule whose two authorities point opposite ways, so the item is not resolved in any operative sense. This is not stylistic: it is a named, load-bearing rule with a security consequence, and the run already spent a reconciliation cycle (fb-09) getting it right in the other two documents.
