## `spec-service-surface` restates the redirect-fragment rule incompatibly with both siblings, and its version deletes the key

**Unit:** `spec-service-surface`. **Criterion:** 5, plus the unit's "Out of scope" clause "**Redirects Relic itself issues are its item, not yours**" (assigned to `spec-viewer`).

**File and line:** `docs/spec/service.md` line 213, first paragraph of section 6.

### The contradiction

Three documents state the same rule. Two agree; `service.md` is the outlier and its version breaks the product.

`docs/spec/format.md` line 239 (section 5) and `docs/spec/viewer.md` lines 79 to 90 (section 1.7) both split the rule on **the destination's trust boundary, explicitly not on the origin tuple**, and both name HTTP-to-HTTPS and apex-to-`www` as cases where the fragment is **deliberately omitted** so inheritance carries the key through. `viewer.md` line 88 states it directly: "applying the other half to them would kill the key on every link typed or pasted without a `www` or a scheme. **The test is the destination's trust boundary, not its origin tuple.**"

`service.md` line 213 keys the MUST on the origin tuple instead: "Every redirect the service issues **across an origin boundary** MUST carry an explicit, possibly empty, fragment in `Location`." It then enumerates where it bites as "**HTTP-to-HTTPS upgrade, apex to `www`**, trailing-slash normalization, and any redirect configured in a CDN or load balancer." The only carve-out it offers is "**A same-origin** canonicalizing redirect is safe on the fragment," which does not reach either case, because a scheme change and a host change both change the origin.

So `service.md` mandates an explicit, possibly empty fragment on exactly the two redirects the other two documents mandate it be omitted from. Implemented as written, a recipient who types or pastes the URL without a scheme or without `www` is redirected to a `Location` carrying an empty fragment, the key is deleted, and they land on `viewer.md` section 6.1's "link is missing its key" screen holding a link that was never broken.

### Why this is a criterion failure and not a wording preference

1. The paragraph is outside this unit's scope. Its "Out of scope" section assigns redirects Relic itself issues to `spec-viewer`, and `spec-viewer` carries them correctly. `service.md` section 6's own mandate is third-party rewriters (link shorteners, Safe Links, Proofpoint), which it covers well.
2. It restates a rule `format.md` settled, in a form that contradicts it. A sibling redefining what another settled is a scope violation.
3. The RFC scoping supports the siblings, not `service.md`. RFC 9110 section 17.11 scopes its remedy to redirects "to other sites"; verified verbatim against the raw RFC.

### Provenance, which explains it

Commit `e2420ae` ("spec: scope format.md section 5 redirect rule to the trust boundary") was the fb-09 fix and it touched **only** `docs/spec/format.md`. `service.md` landed at `ed51b43`, before that fix, and was never reconciled. `viewer.md` (`7121fc2`) reached the correct split independently through its own adversary pass. `service.md` is the single un-reconciled copy.

### What would make it pass

Either:

- **Drop the restatement.** Delete the redirect rule from `service.md` line 213 and cite `format.md` section 5 and `viewer.md` section 1.7 for it, keeping only the two consequences this unit genuinely owns: the ID-normalization requirement in the following paragraph (cache keys, cap accounting, log correlation normalize before keying) and the third-party cases from the rest of section 6. This is the cleaner fix, since the unit's scope assigns the rule elsewhere.
- **Or restate it compatibly.** Replace "across an origin boundary" with the destination's-trust-boundary test the two siblings use, move HTTP-to-HTTPS and apex-to-`www` out of the "where it bites" list and into the fragment-omitted half, and widen the carve-out from "same-origin" to "anywhere inside the service, including its host and scheme variants."

Either way `service.md` must not be the document a `build` implementer reads to learn that an HTTP-to-HTTPS redirect carries an empty fragment.
