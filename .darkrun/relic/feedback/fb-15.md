FIT-2. The hard size cap value and its referent is routed by three of the four spec documents, named in no unit body, and swept into a unit that cannot decide it.

The same decision is routed three times: `format.md` §4.4 ("Whether the cap is on plaintext or ciphertext, and its value"), `publish.md` §6.3 ("The size cap value and its referent, plaintext or ciphertext"), and `viewer.md` §7.2 ("The hard size cap value"). Grep of all five unit files for "size cap", "cap value", "referent", and "plaintext or ciphertext" returns zero hits. No unit body names it.

Two sweep criteria claim it and they claim it for different units. `design-container-and-crypto` criterion 10 sweeps `format.md` §4, which includes §4.4. `design-topology-and-origins` criterion 11 sweeps `viewer.md` §7, which includes §7.2. So on paper two units in different waves each own the same value, and in practice neither is set up to pick it:

- **Container is not instructed to read the document holding the binding constraint.** Its read list is `frame.md`, `preconditions.md`, `format.md`, `viewer.md` on decryption and platform ceilings, `publish.md` on retry and re-encryption. It never reads `service.md`, and `service.md` §2.3 is where the arithmetic lives: "a floor of 40 legitimate mints and a ceiling near 80 where scanners detonate... The value is `shape`'s and this is its binding constraint." Container is also wave 1, so it has no sibling output to lean on.
- **Topology has no cap arithmetic in its charter at all**, and its own out-of-scope line assigns "cost arithmetic" to `design-storage-grant-and-cost`.
- **Storage, the unit that actually holds the egress arithmetic**, decides "the per-object download cap and the per-IP publish quota" and never the size cap. Its out-of-scope line says the container format is "Locked by `docs/design/container.md`", which invites it to treat a container-decided cap as settled, or to redecide it, with nothing to arbitrate.

This is the routed item with the largest structural blast radius after the container framing itself. `viewer.md` §7.2 states it plainly: the cap "determines whether §5's tiering is required at all: a cap below the in-memory ceiling collapses three tiers into one." `format.md` §4.4 adds "the grant's signed size constraint and the worst-case egress arithmetic in the preconditions." A cap arrived at by omission either builds a three-tier streaming viewer that was never needed or forecloses one that was, and it lands inside the signed grant construction. That is expensive structural reversal, which is the risk this station exists to kill.

The same sweep-without-body pattern leaves two more `format.md` §4 items resting on container's criterion 10 alone:

- **§4.3, the ID entropy bit count.** Container's body never mentions it and its "already decided, do not relitigate" list does not cover it. `design-operations-and-abuse` §5 records enumeration as settled "at the entropy floor already fixed," which reads the floor as the answer when `format.md` §1.2 makes 122 bits a floor and the number `shape`'s.
- **§4.6, whether object metadata is set at upload time at all.** Also routed identically as `publish.md` §6.5. Container's body never mentions metadata. Storage's body touches it only as a probe input ("can client metadata be injected on the unsigned leg") and as a defect in the resumable candidate. Neither is told to decide whether any metadata is needed, which is what both spec documents say is genuinely open.

**What would make this pass:** name the size cap value and its referent in exactly one unit body, with the unit given the reading that constrains it (`service.md` §2.3 for the arithmetic, `viewer.md` §5 for the tiering consequence, `format.md` §3.11 for the referent constraint), and remove it from the other unit's sweep so two documents cannot decide it. Do the same for §4.3 and §4.6: a body instruction in one unit, not a sweep criterion in a unit whose body is silent.
