FIT-3. `design-topology-and-origins` criterion 11 sweeps `viewer.md` §7 into the wrong unit, and collides head-on with `design-product-surface`.

Topology's criterion 11 reads: "Every decision routed here by `viewer.md` §7 is decided with its consequence stated, or explicitly eliminated with the reason." `viewer.md` §7 routes five items. Only one of them, §7.5 PSL registration for the sandbox parent, is inside topology's charter and named in its body (§3, the domain workstream). The other four are not:

- **§7.1 platform memory ceilings, hardcoded or feature-detected.** Topology's body never mentions memory. This is a viewer degradation decision resting on `viewer.md` §5, which topology is not told to read (its read list is `viewer.md` §§1.7, 2, 4, 7).
- **§7.2 the hard size cap value.** Covered separately in FIT-2.
- **§7.3 the truncated-prefix size**, which `viewer.md` §7.3 notes "the viewer states in its own copy." That is recipient-facing copy, which `design-product-surface` owns.
- **§7.4 the highlighted-region cap**, also user-visible. **This is a direct collision.** `design-product-surface` §4 names it explicitly: "the highlighted-region cap routed by `viewer.md` is about CPU rather than bytes. Keep those separate and decide both." So surface's body is instructed to decide it and topology's criterion 11 requires topology to decide it too. Two documents deciding the same thing is the exact failure this review exists to catch, and it is worse here than a silent gap because surface runs after topology and inherits topology's out-of-scope line "Origins, edge, TLS, the mint trigger. Locked by `docs/design/topology.md`," which does not tell it what to do with a highlight cap topology already wrote down.

Topology's own out-of-scope section proves the mismatch: it hands "Viewer screens, copy, and art direction" to surface, and criterion 11 then requires it to decide two user-visible viewer values and a memory ceiling.

The mirror-image problem is that **surface has no sweep criterion at all**. Its twelve criteria cover the art direction, the CSP font and icon constraint, the three missing states, `mints_remaining`, the taskbar, the renderer stack, quotes, and dashes. Nothing of the form "every decision routed here by `viewer.md` §7 is decided." So the three viewer items that genuinely belong to surface (§7.1, §7.3, §7.4) are enforced only against the unit that should not have them.

**What would make this pass:** move `viewer.md` §7.1, §7.3, and §7.4 into `design-product-surface`'s body and give surface the sweep criterion, keeping §7.5 with topology and re-scoping topology's criterion 11 to the item it actually owns. Then delete the instruction to decide the highlight cap from whichever unit does not keep it, so exactly one document decides it.
