FIT-4. `design-storage-grant-and-cost` cannot pick the download cap without `design-topology-and-origins`'s mint-trigger decision, and the two run in the same wave with no declared edge.

Declared graph: container has no dependencies; topology and storage both depend on container only; surface depends on container and topology; operations depends on storage and topology. So topology and storage run in parallel, and `depends_on` is the only thing the scheduler sequences on.

Storage §2 is told to "Decide the per-object download cap and the per-IP publish quota, showing the arithmetic." The arithmetic it must show is `service.md` §2.3's, which reads: "Section 2's shell rule removes the non-executing scans, leaving a floor of 40 legitimate mints and a ceiling near 80 where scanners detonate with a browser." **Whether scanners detonate is exactly topology §4's decision**, which reads: "Decide: auto-mint on load, or mint behind a signal a headless previewer does not produce." Gate the mint and the ceiling collapses toward the floor of 40; leave it on load and the cap has to absorb up to 80 mints per relic before a human clicks, which roughly doubles the number and the worst-case egress it feeds.

Topology's own body concedes the coupling and then does nothing about it: "Auto-on-load is cheap now and hard to change once the open counter has a baseline and **the cap has a published value**." That sentence names storage's output as the thing that locks topology's decision, while storage's number is derived from topology's decision. The edge is real in one direction and stated in neither unit's `depends_on`.

Two ways this ships broken. Storage picks a cap sized for scanner detonation and topology gates the mint, so the published cap is roughly twice what the traffic needs and the cost control is slack in exactly the dimension `preconditions.md` calls the go/no-go. Or storage picks a cap sized for 40, topology leaves auto-mint on load, and ordinary email distribution to a 40-person Defender tenant exhausts the cap before anyone opens the link, which `service.md` §2.3 already warns "breaks ordinary email distribution on legitimate traffic alone." Neither unit's completion criteria catch it: topology's criterion 8 only requires the mint decision and the cost of the branch not taken, and storage has no criterion on the cap value at all.

The same undeclared edge propagates to `design-operations-and-abuse` §5, which is told that "Scanners can exhaust a cap before a human opens the link, producing a ticket indistinguishable from 'the operator deleted my file.'" Whether that is even possible is topology §4's answer. Operations does depend on topology, so it inherits correctly; storage does not.

**What would make this pass:** add `design-topology-and-origins` to `design-storage-grant-and-cost`'s `depends_on` and inputs, so the mint trigger is settled before the cap arithmetic runs, and state in storage's body that the cap is computed against the mint-trigger branch topology chose. If the sequencing cost is unacceptable, the alternative is to move the cap value into topology alongside the mint trigger, but splitting them across parallel units is the one arrangement that cannot work.
