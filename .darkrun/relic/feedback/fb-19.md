## simplicity review: the unit count is right, the routing coverage inside it is not

**Verdict on five: keep five.** The defect is not the decomposition. It is that the completion criteria were written per-unit as "everything routed by document X" without checking that document X's routed list matches that unit's subject. Two of the four routing sections got a catch-all, two got none, and the catch-alls that exist point at the wrong documents. Result: **3 of the 23 routed decisions have no owner anywhere, and 2 are owned in two places.** Every claim below is grep-checkable.

### 1. Three routed decisions have no owner in any unit body or any completion criterion

`grep -Ein "proof.of.work|difficulty|dedup|backoff|retry cap" .darkrun/relic/units/design-*.md` returns **zero hits across all five units**.

- **`publish.md` §6.2, proof of work and its difficulty.** Unowned. Lowest stakes of the three: §6.2 itself says "The wire shape is fixed in 3.1 so this is a value rather than a protocol change."
- **`publish.md` §6.4, the retry cap and backoff bounds.** Unowned, and coupled: §6.4 says "Once picked: ... how much egress a single failing publish can consume." `design-storage-grant-and-cost` is computing the egress ceiling and the kill-switch residual without the number that bounds a single failing publish.
- **`service.md` §7.7, the mint dedup interval.** Unowned, and this is the one that matters. `service.md` §2.2 makes the interval decide whether a repeated successful mint from the same IP is a distinct open, and notes it "interacts with the frame's 120-second window." `design-topology-and-origins` §4 decides whether the mint fires on load or behind a gesture, and its entire phantom-open argument is a counting argument. The interval that governs the counting is not assigned to topology or to anyone else.

Nothing catches these at close. The brief's "Done when" reads "every **assigned** routed decision," so an unassigned one closes the station silently.

Softer fourth case: **`service.md` §7.6, the published SLA in hours.** `design-operations-and-abuse` §6 names it as a commitment line item, and criterion 10 only requires "an itemized list the operator can answer." A list that says "the published SLA in hours" satisfies the criterion without ever picking a number. `service.md` routes a number.

### 2. Two decisions are owned in two places

This is the defect class, not benign restatement. (Benign duplication is everywhere and correctly so: the five-citation-defects warning, "never WebFetch a specification," the sibling-obligation rule, and the style block appear in all five bodies because each body is its agent's only context. Those are not findings.)

- **The hard size cap value and its referent.** Routed three times: `format.md` §4.4 ("Whether the cap is on plaintext or ciphertext, and its value"), `viewer.md` §7.2 ("The hard size cap value"), `publish.md` §6.3 ("The size cap value and its referent"). Container criterion 10 claims all of `format.md` §4, so container.md owns it. Topology criterion 11 claims all of `viewer.md` §7, so topology.md owns it too. **Neither body mentions the cap.** Meanwhile `design-storage-grant-and-cost`, whose §1 hangs the entire grant decision tree on "a signed size constraint," only *consumes* the cap and never decides it. `publish.md` §6.3 says once picked it fixes `size_limit_bytes`, `size_basis`, the client pre-check in 1.2, and the signed constraint in 3.6. One number, two nominal owners who were not told about it, and the document that needs it owns nothing.
- **The highlighted-region cap.** `viewer.md` §7.4. Topology criterion 11 claims it. `design-product-surface` §4 also claims it explicitly: "the highlighted-region cap routed by `viewer.md` is about CPU rather than bytes. Keep those separate and decide both." Two owners, and the units run in different waves.

### 3. Under-specification: catch-alls demanding decisions the body never sets up

`design-topology-and-origins` criterion 11 requires deciding **every** `viewer.md` §7 item. Its body decides exactly one of the five (§7.5, the PSL). The other four:

- §7.1 platform memory ceilings, which `viewer.md` §7.1 grounds in §5
- §7.2 the hard size cap
- §7.3 the truncated-prefix size, which is viewer copy
- §7.4 the highlighted-region cap, which `viewer.md` §7.4 grounds in §3.3

Topology's read list is `viewer.md` "§1.7 redirects, §2 the sandbox origin, §4 the boundary, §7 routed items." **§5 and §3.3 are not in it.** The agent is required by criterion to decide four items its body is silent on, from sections it was not told to read, and three of the four are the viewer document's subject matter, which is `design-product-surface`.

Same shape, smaller, in container: criterion 10 carries `format.md` §4.6, "Whether object metadata is set at upload time at all," and the container body never mentions metadata. The evidence for that decision lives in `design-storage-grant-and-cost`'s body, which discusses `X-Goog-Meta-*` on the unsigned resumable leg as a defect of a grant candidate. Assignment sits in one unit, evidence in another, and container is not pointed at the GCS knowledge topic.

### 4. What is not a finding, stated so it is not re-raised

- **Length.** Density held. Container: 22 decided items (11 decision bullets plus 11 criteria) over 1,550 words is 70 words per item. Storage: 23 over 1,476 is 64. Both inside the 65 to 71 band the previous station measured. I found no passage worth naming as padding. The nearest miss is one sentence in `design-product-surface` §5 about optional display title and icon members on a tool definition, which forecloses acting on itself ("a hint and never a control") and carries no criterion, but one sentence is not a defect.
- **The four gates per unit are all executable and all fail correctly** in an isolated worktree from repo root. `sources-manifest-populated` errors under `set -eu` on a missing manifest; `every-cited-url-resolves` fails on a dead URL and its vacuous-pass-on-empty case is closed by the preceding gate. The known hole is the one the units already state themselves: all five `specify` citation defects had resolving URLs. That is why criterion 9/10/11's verbatim-quote audit exists, and it is prose rather than a gate by necessity.
- **Two deterministic checks sit outside the gate set as prose criteria:** `grep -c '[—–]'` (every unit) and `grep -c 'AES-128'` (container criterion 5). Both are mechanical and free. If either is promoted to a gate, note that `grep -c` **exits 1 when the count is 0**, so the em-dash check must be written `test "$(grep -c '[—–]' <file>)" -eq 0` or `! grep -q`, not as the criterion's current phrasing.

### The fix does not require re-decomposing

Five units are the right five. The four irreversibility classes the brief names map one-to-one onto container, topology, storage, and the mint trigger inside topology, with operations pricing the go/no-go. The merges available are all bad trades: folding storage into container would put the run's only irreversible decision inside its largest document, and splitting product-surface for the one MCP publishing-moment decision costs a wave to move a paragraph. The dependency graph is 3 waves at max width 2, and the only edge I cannot find a stated justification for is `design-topology-and-origins` depending on `design-container-and-crypto` (storage's dependency is justified in its body as "fixes the overhead arithmetic your size math depends on"; topology's has no equivalent). Dropping that edge would not shorten the makespan, so it is an observation, not a request.

This is criterion-level work: give `design-storage-grant-and-cost` a catch-all for `publish.md` §6 and the storage-side `service.md` §7 items, put the three orphans with a named owner, and reconcile the two existing catch-alls so `viewer.md` §7's viewer-facing items sit with the viewer document rather than with topology. Unit count, unit bodies, and the dependency graph can all stand.
