# reversibility review of the `shape` decomposition

Four findings. The first blocks `design-topology-and-origins`, which runs second. The rest are ordered by reversal cost.

## 1. BLOCKER. The PSL foreclosure removes the stated rationale for per-relic subdomains, and the collision is not routed as drift

`viewer.md` 2 is locked and says, verbatim: "**PSL registration of the sandbox parent is required and routes to `shape`**". The station spec forecloses it. That much is known. What nobody carried back is what the same locked section rests the per-relic subdomain decision on.

`viewer.md` 2 concedes the obvious objection is not real ("every rendered document already sits in its own opaque origin, so two relics on a single hostname are already mutually cross-origin"), then states the actual benefit: "**What per-relic subdomains actually buy is process-level isolation**", and quotes web.dev for the mechanism: "by adding `exampleusercontent.com` to the PSL, you can ensure that `foo.exampleusercontent.com` and `bar.exampleusercontent.com` are cross-site and thus fully isolated from each other."

The PSL entry is what makes the labels cross-site. Process isolation keys on site, not origin. Without the entry, every per-relic label shares one registrable domain, is same-site, and buys no process isolation. The locked document states its own dependency and the station has foreclosed it.

What is being carried forward on that rationale is the most expensive structural commitment in the topology unit: a wildcard certificate, DNS-01 and therefore DNS provider API credentials wherever issuance runs, an edge with a standing monthly cost after Cloud Run domain mappings are excluded, and unbounded auto-generated hostnames under a wildcard, which `viewer.md` 2 itself names as "exactly what `preconditions.md` 2 names as the trigger" for the Immich pattern. All of it baked into the URL scheme and the CSP.

`design-topology-and-origins` treats per-relic subdomains as settled and asks only how to serve them. Its criterion 6 forces the document to say the PSL is foreclosed and that a PSL entry would not break the wildcard. Both are true and neither makes the writer notice the rationale is gone. The document will pass its criteria with this hole in it.

The station spec's out-of-scope section already has the right mechanism and lists two drift items (the egress figure, the revoke citation). This is a third and it is larger than either. It needs naming as drift back to `specify` before topology decides the edge, because the answer changes whether the wildcard is needed at all.

This is `cross-document-gaps-no-criterion-catches` one level up: there a rule sat in the wrong document; here a finding in one station invalidates a rationale in another, and no criterion reads across them.

## 2. HIGH. The Cloud Run foreclosure is over-broad, and a wrong foreclosure is the expensive reversal in the other direction

The station spec forecloses "**Cloud Run for the per-relic subdomain origin**" and the topology unit repeats it under "What is already foreclosed. Do not design against it."

The evidence does not support that scope. The quote "You cannot use wildcard certificates with this feature" is from the Cloud Run custom domain mappings page, and "this feature" is domain mappings. What is foreclosed is Cloud Run domain mappings as the TLS and fronting layer. Cloud Run as a backend behind a global external Application Load Balancer with a Certificate Manager wildcard is untouched by that sentence, and it is the shape the run's own knowledge already points at: `per-relic-subdomain-topology-wildcard-tls-psl-and-hsts` says the remaining shapes are "a global external Application Load Balancer plus Certificate Manager, which does support wildcards under DNS authorization, or a non-GCP edge", and says nothing about what sits behind the ALB. The app server is already on Cloud Run throughout `gcs-cloud-run-architecture-constraints` and `relic-stack-options-and-what-each-forecloses`. Confirmed: "Cloud Run" appears zero times across all four locked spec documents, so this foreclosure enters the run for the first time in the station spec and the unit, where it becomes binding.

The over-broad reading pushes the writer toward the third-party edge. The unit itself names that cost: "a third party in the render path" and "what a third party in the render path means for a zero-knowledge claim, because that is the honest cost and it belongs in the disclosure statement". Once that lands in a published disclosure statement, backing it out is a public revision of the zero-knowledge posture. That is a real reversal cost incurred for a foreclosure that is not real.

This is the exact pattern `gcs-false-impossibility-claims` records, one layer up from GCS: "asserting an impossibility where the real answer is a cost or a design choice", and its stated reason for why it matters applies verbatim, that a wrong impossibility claim "closes a question the next station would otherwise have to answer, and nobody reopens a question the spec says is settled."

Narrowing the foreclosure to the domain-mapping feature costs one clause. Leaving it broad costs an edge decision.

## 3. HIGH. Twelve of the twenty-three routed decisions have no forcing criterion, and four of the expensive ones are among them

The station spec's claim that "The spec set routes **23 decisions** here" checks out: `format.md` 4 routes six, `viewer.md` 7 routes five, `publish.md` 6 routes five, `service.md` 7 routes seven.

Two units carry a catch-all criterion. `design-container-and-crypto` criterion 10 covers every `format.md` 4 item. `design-topology-and-origins` criterion 11 covers every `viewer.md` 7 item. That is eleven. **The eleven routed by `publish.md` 6 and `service.md` 7 have no catch-all criterion anywhere**, because `design-storage-grant-and-cost`, `design-product-surface`, and `design-operations-and-abuse` have none. Storage reads both of those routed-item sections by name in its inputs and has criteria only for the grant, the probes, the tree, spend caps, key rotation, quotes, and the project branches.

Five routed items appear in no unit's prose at all: `publish.md` 6.2 (proof of work and its difficulty), `publish.md` 6.4 (retry cap and backoff bounds), `service.md` 7.7 (the mint dedup interval), `viewer.md` 7.1 (platform memory ceilings), `viewer.md` 7.3 (the truncated-prefix size). Verified by grep across all five unit files.

Four of the unforced items are directly in this station's own risk:

- **The soft-delete posture and the retention window** (`service.md` 7.3, 7.5). `preconditions.md` states the reversal property in its own words: "setting it late leaves a tail nobody can retroactively clear, and it has to match the retention window published below. That is what makes it a precondition rather than an operational detail." This is a decision that is free today, invisible tomorrow, and permanently unfixable afterward, which is the station's risk definition exactly. It lives in one clause of storage section 4 prose and no criterion touches section 4.
- **The TTL ceiling** (`service.md` 7.3). Same clause, same absence. It also bounds finding 5 below.
- **The per-object download cap value** (`service.md` 7.2). The station forces the mint-trigger decision (topology criterion 8) on the argument that a gate "gets much harder to add once the open counter has a baseline and **the cap has a published value**". The station forces the decision that depends on the number and does not force the number.
- **The mint dedup interval** (`service.md` 7.7), which `service.md` says "interacts with the frame's 120-second window". This is a counting rule whose value cannot be re-derived once a series exists, the same failure mode as the mint trigger, and it is in no unit.

The `viewer.md` 7 criterion has a second problem worth noting while this is open: it sits on `design-topology-and-origins`, whose out-of-scope section assigns viewer concerns to `design-product-surface`. Items 1 through 4 of `viewer.md` 7 are memory ceilings, the size cap value, the truncated-prefix size, and the highlighted-region cap. Only item 5 (PSL) is topology's. The writer will either write out of scope or fail criterion 11.

## 4. MEDIUM-HIGH. The name is the only operator decision with no unit and no criterion

Of the five operator decisions the station spec enumerates, four have a home and a forcing criterion: the abuse commitment (operations criterion 10), separate GCP projects (storage criterion 11), EU exposure (inside operations section 6's enumerated list), whether to run the probes (storage criterion 6). **The name has neither.** `relic-name-is-crowded-in-its-own-three-categories` is in `design-product-surface`'s read list, and nothing in that unit's decisions or criteria asks for the naming decision to be stated. Verified by grep: no unit mentions the collision, the npm package, or renaming as a decision.

The one place it surfaces is `design-container-and-crypto` line 30, which de-ranks it in passing: "Everything else can be migrated, redeployed, or **renamed**." That is true today and false at the next step, and it is the only sentence in the decomposition that touches the subject.

The station spec is right that renaming is "free now and brutal after domains are bought and links are in the wild", and the knowledge topic adds the sequencing that makes it urgent: "The domain purchase is already a stated external dependency blocking deployment, so the naming decision sits immediately upstream of it." Zero cost today, closing at the very next external step, no owner.

## Reversal-cost ranking, with what moved and why

The organizing fact nobody has stated: **the mandatory TTL is the universal reversal bound for this system.** Anything whose cost lives in stored objects ages out within one TTL. Only five things escape it: what is baked into a domain, what runs on an external clock nobody controls, reputation accrued on a domain, data you did not retain or cannot unsee, and a counter series already published.

**Tier 1, not bounded by TTL:**

1. **The name, and therefore the domains.** Not on the spec's list of four. Unbounded because the frame locks "no accounts", so there is no channel to tell an installed MCP client fleet the domain moved, only the package registry. HSTS preload and Search Console and Safe Browsing standing all restart from zero on a new domain. Free today. Moved up from unranked.
2. **HSTS preload.** Months in, months out, unexpeditable, and `includeSubDomains` makes a valid certificate on every per-relic hostname a permanent availability dependency. Not on the spec's four. The topology unit covers it properly at section 3 with criterion 7, so the decomposition is ahead of the spec's own ranking here. Moved up from unranked.
3. **Which origin serves the download path.** Keep it high, fix the reason. The spec says "Baked into the URL scheme and the CSP." Both of those are cheap to change: a header and a link target. What does not migrate is the Safe Browsing download-category history accrued on the domain that is in every shared link and cannot be replaced. Reputation is the irreversible term, not the URL scheme, and the stated reason drives what the writer optimizes. Separately, the unit's option set is "service origin or sandbox origin" while `safe-browsing-delisting-and-why-a-zero-knowledge-operator-cannot-comply` records a third path the unit does not enumerate, "a `blob:` save of already-decrypted content is a different path from a direct link to the object", which matters because the categories attach to whichever origin serves bytes to disk.
4. **The soft-delete posture and the retention window.** Not on the spec's four. Permanently unclearable if set late, in `preconditions.md`'s own words. No forcing criterion. Moved up from unranked.

**Tier 2, bounded by one TTL:**

5. **The container framing.** Correctly the spec's number one on per-object cost, where the loss is total and permanent. But the spec's own de-escalation is right, and it has a consequence nobody drew: because the fragment marker is pre-fetch, the system-level cost is dual decoders for one TTL, so the framing is TTL-bounded while items 1 through 4 are not. **The bound holds only if the TTL ceiling is actually decided, and per finding 3 no criterion forces it.** The station's strongest reassurance rests on a number no unit has to produce.
6. **The mint trigger.** Correctly identified, correct reasoning, forced by topology criterion 8. The technical reversal is a client-side deploy; the expensive part is the counter baseline and the published cap, which is exactly what the spec says. Note the frame softens it slightly: the metric's first clause is not reported below roughly 100 relics per week or while one publishing IP dominates, so there is a window where the baseline does not yet matter. The published cap value is the harder half, and it is unforced.
7. **The GCP project topology.** **Moved down from the spec's number three.** As a reversal item it is bounded: object URLs are minted at view time so no bucket name is in any published link, and one TTL of dual-bucket operation drains the old bucket. The spec's stated reason ("moving buckets and signing identities later is a migration rather than a config change") is the weak version. Its real weight is blast radius, which is a risk-exposure argument, not a reversal argument, and it is already an explicit operator decision with a forcing criterion (storage criterion 11), so it cannot be decided by omission. The one genuine reversal argument the spec does not make: the migration is cheap when you do not need it and impossible at the moment you do, because a suspended project cannot be migrated out of. Keep it high on that reason, not the stated one.
8. **The grant shape.** Correctly absent from the list, and the station should not be tempted to add it. Its one irreversible-looking tail is that objects written on the resumable branch carry unpinnable metadata permanently, since the data leg is unsigned, and changing the branch later fixes only new objects. That tail is TTL-bounded like everything else in storage. The grant shape is a correctness problem, not a reversal problem, and the decomposition treats it correctly.

**Correctly demoted, nothing to add:**

9. **`rs`.** The reclassification to Tier 3 is right. It lives in each object's plaintext header, the RFC and `wormhole-crypto` both bear that out, and the condition that makes it cheap (the reader parses `rs` from the header rather than a compiled-in default) is enforced by container criterion 6 rather than left as prose. This is the model for how the other reclassifications should have been handled.

## On the grant-shape decision tree, judged as asked

**It is a legitimate structure, not a deferral wearing a decision's clothes.** The locked routing authorizes it directly. `publish.md` 6.1 already concedes the requirements "are not aligned across the candidates" and calls reconciling them "the decision", so the station is authorized to choose which requirement gives. And `publish.md` 6.1 already mandates the sequencing: "**The size check in 3.6 runs against a prototype grant before the resumable or signed-header branches are committed**, because a negative result there eliminates a branch rather than filing a bug." A tree keyed on unrun probes is what the locked document asked for, not an evasion invented here.

One precision gap, offered as a note rather than a new requirement. Criterion 7 requires "no leaf is left undecided", and the unit describes leaves as "branch choices". A leaf that names a candidate has not stated which of the four requirements it sacrifices, and since three of the four are locked spec sentences (`publish.md` 3.6, 3.7, 3.4, 4.4), a leaf that sacrifices one is a drift routing rather than a decision the station can take unilaterally. As written, criterion 7 passes on a tree that names candidates and leaves the sacrifice implicit, which is the part that would have to be reopened later.

## Foreclosure verification, all four

- **Public Suffix List: real, but the scope is narrower than stated and the consequence was not carried.** The decline criteria, the candour requirement, the two-year term, and the propagation timescale are all as recorded. The honest scope is "unavailable at launch at this scale", not permanently foreclosed, since the size criterion is a scale gate. The failure is finding 1, not the foreclosure itself.
- **Cloud Run: not real as stated.** See finding 2.
- **Safe Browsing appeal as a mitigation: real, and correctly scoped.** Canonicalization strips the fragment before anything else, so the sample URL is the one form that cannot open the content, and confirm-fix-document is unanswerable. Note the foreclosure is correctly on "a mitigation the design leans on" and not on appeals in general, since the Immich appeal was accepted in "a day or two" and then recurred. The operations unit handles it right by specifying the reconsideration artifact as a pre-listing deliverable.
- **Any control keyed on User-Agent: real.** Signal's Android client sets `WhatsApp/2` in its own published source, which settles it for a control. "Control" is the right word; the evidence establishes UA is unreliable for identification, not that it is useless as one signal among several, and nothing in the decomposition depends on the stronger reading.
- **The trap check, "a PSL entry would not break the wildcard": correct.** BR 3.2.2.6 directs CAs to the ICANN section only, private entries go in the PRIVATE section, and Let's Encrypt confirmed it on the record. Two caveats the knowledge topic carries and the station spec drops: it is a SHOULD rather than a MUST so it is CA-dependent, and the applicant here controls the entire namespace, which is the BR's own escape hatch anyway. Low impact given the PSL is unavailable, but the SHOULD should travel with the claim so it is not later read as a guarantee.

## Not filed

The unrun probes. Stated, with the reason, and `publish.md` 6.1 asks for exactly this sequencing.
