FIT-8. Regression introduced by the fb-15 fix. `design-container-and-crypto` §6, newly added to give `format.md` 4.3 an owner, encodes the ID under the wrong alphabet and points the wrong check at it.

The section reads: "Decide the bit count and state both costs: the ID's length in characters **under 2.3's unpadded base64url encoding**, which `format.md` 1.5 makes the primary reserved-word guard, against the enumeration arithmetic at the value you choose. **Apply the same terminal-character check §1 applies to the fragment.**"

Both halves are wrong against the locked document, and `format.md` keeps the two encodings deliberately apart.

**1. The ID is Crockford base32, not base64url.** `format.md` 1.1: "**Crockford base32, lowercase canonical, case-insensitive on lookup**." The same section states why the two differ and says so explicitly: "The key stays base64url because a 43-character key isn't worth optimizing for hand transcription." 2.3 is the *key's* encoding, and its opening line is "The key is **unpadded base64url**." 1.2 gives the ID arithmetic directly: "**At Crockford's 5 bits per character, a 122-bit floor is 25 characters, and that number is what 1.5's primary guard rests on.**"

**The consequence is concrete and it lands on a locked guarantee.** At the 125 bits this unit's beat decided, Crockford base32 gives 25 characters and base64url gives 21. `format.md` 1.5 states: "IDs are exactly N characters and 1.2 floors N at 25. The longest reserved word is `manifest.webmanifest` at 20, so length excludes every word in the table by at least five characters under the weakest entropy this document permits. **No `shape` decision weakens it, because the floor is the floor.**" A 21-character ID is below the stated floor of 25 and clears the longest reserved word by one character rather than five, so following this instruction breaks the primary reserved-word guard and falsifies a sentence in a locked document. `/abuse` is a go/no-go obligation in the preconditions, and 1.5 notes an issued ID shadowing a reserved route makes the relic "disappear with no error anywhere."

**2. The terminal-character check does not apply to the ID.** The `fragment-terminal-charset` check in 2.3 exists because base64url can emit `_`, which GFM's autolink extension trims from a trailing position. Crockford base32's alphabet carries no punctuation at all, so the intersection with GFM's trailing-punctuation set is empty by construction and the check is vacuous rather than merely misplaced. 2.3's own arithmetic is scoped to the key throughout, and the URL's terminal character is the last character of the key, not the ID.

**Why this is worth fixing even though the beat already ran.** The designer beat produced "125 bits, 25 Crockford characters," which is right, so it did not follow the instruction. But the beat's own note ranks this decision fourth on what the challenge pass should attack, calling 125 over 122 "aesthetics dressed as arithmetic" picked on encoding alignment. So this text is live for exactly the pass most likely to re-derive the number, with an instruction that yields 21 characters sitting in it. No completion criterion catches it: criterion 10 only requires 4.3 decided with its consequence stated.

**What would make this pass:** in §6, replace "under 2.3's unpadded base64url encoding" with 1.1's Crockford base32 at 5 bits per character, cite 1.2's own statement of the arithmetic, and drop the terminal-character sentence or state that the check is 2.3's and applies to the key rather than the ID.
