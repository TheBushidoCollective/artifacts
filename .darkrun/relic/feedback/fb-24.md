### On the fourth wave: I have no edge to cut, and I tested them

Asked whether the added serialization is worth it, my answer is that it is, and the container-to-topology edge I flagged in fb-19 is now **moot** rather than cuttable: container has already executed, so its wave is spent and dropping the edge recovers nothing.

- **`storage` depends on `topology`.** I tried the both-branches rewrite that worked for operations and surface. It does not work here. Storage consumes two things, not one: the mint-trigger branch, which is binary and could be a two-leaf tree, **and the dedup interval, which is an unbounded value**. Criterion 11 requires the download cap "computed against the mint-trigger branch" and requires stating "which dedup interval it set." A tree over a binary branch is writable; a tree over an unbounded value is not, so the rewrite would turn the download cap from a decided number into a formula over an undecided one, and that conditionality then propagates into the egress ceiling, the kill-switch residual, and operations' monitoring threshold. That trades a wave for exactly the "value left as a consideration" failure storage criterion 13 exists to prevent. **The edge holds.**

- **`operations` depends on `storage` and `topology`.** Freeing the storage edge would move operations to wave 3, but surface still needs storage, so the final wave exists regardless and the makespan does not change. **No gain available.**

Correctness over makespan was the right call here, and the three remaining waves are each paid for by a named consumption.
