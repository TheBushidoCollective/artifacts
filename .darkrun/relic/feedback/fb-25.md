Unit `design-product-surface`, artifact `docs/design/surface.md` section 5 (lines 135-151). The in-memory ceiling decision rests on an empirical claim nobody measured, and unlike this station's other unproven-empirical decisions, nothing defers the measurement to Build.

The decision: "the in-memory ceiling is hardcoded at 500,000,000 octets... it is not feature-detected." Paired with storage.md's 100 MiB cap, this collapses viewer.md section 5's three-tier streaming architecture into one tier. The document itself names this "the largest structural consequence in this station after the container framing," and states plainly what reversal costs: "raising the size cap later is not a configuration change, it is building tier 1 and every ServiceWorker rule viewer.md 5 attaches to it."

What the 500,000,000 number rests on: the document's own words, "the bottom of the 500 to 800 MB band, which that section correctly records as traceable to one forum thread reporting an OperationError at 800 MB. It is a practitioner report and not a vendor limit, read at its most conservative point." No source URL for this figure appears in surface.sources.txt. It is inherited from viewer.md 7.1 (a locked document) and never independently re-measured by this unit. Nobody in the challenge/resolve loop flagged it: pressure_tester's six defects don't touch it, and the resolver's own closing "residual risk Build inherits" list names three other open items (icon-fetch timing, taskbar at true phone widths, unsourced font byte counts) but not this one.

Contrast with how this same station handled its other unproven empirical claims:
- `design-storage-grant-and-cost` section 2.2 specifies five probes for the grant shape and states explicitly "these were not run... executing them is the first task of build, ahead of committing the branch." Nothing is locked to a guess.
- `design-topology-and-origins` ran an actual headless-Chrome-over-CDP test to check the trusted-input claim rather than asserting it.
- `design-container-and-crypto` reproduced RFC 8188 test vectors byte-for-byte on Node, independently, twice.

The memory ceiling gets none of that treatment. It is asserted as a hardcoded architectural constant, foreclosing tier 1 for v1, on the strength of one anecdotal forum post read at its most conservative point, with no validation step assigned to Build and no residual-risk flag naming that one is needed. The 1.57x margin at the worst-case peak (319,208,240 octets against the 500,000,000 ceiling) is real but does not substitute for measuring the actual per-tab ceiling, particularly given the taskbar's own primary targets are named phones (375px iPhone SE, 393px iPhone 15), and mobile Safari has historically carried tighter per-tab memory limits than desktop, which is exactly the population most exposed if the number is wrong.

Requested fix, matching the pattern storage.md already set: name an explicit pre-Build (or first-task-of-Build) empirical validation step, measuring actual OperationError/crash thresholds for the full four-copy worst case across target engines, especially mobile Safari, before the single-tier architecture is treated as locked. This does not require reversing the decision, a 500,000,000 hardcoded ceiling may well survive the measurement, it requires stating that the number is unmeasured and assigning who measures it before Build finalizes an architecture that is expensive to unwind if it is wrong.
